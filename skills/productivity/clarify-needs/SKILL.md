# Clarify Needs

Help the user reach a reviewable Needs Summary through an adaptive conversation in their language. Use the loop below where understanding is missing; carry forward settled answers and adjust the order to the situation.

## Establish the Needs Goal

Separate the problem in its real user context, the desired outcome, and any proposed solution. A **Needs Goal** is the user-confirmed outcome with observable success criteria and material constraints; an app or activity is a candidate means. Suggested goals and revisions remain unconfirmed until the user accepts them. When they confirm a change, update the criteria, subsequent reasoning, and summary together.

Advance one important decision per turn. Ask only about gaps that could materially change the goal, options, or judgement of success. If the user cannot answer, offer concrete situations or a few meaningfully different choices they can select or correct; keep recommendations as candidates. Resolve low-impact presentation choices yourself.

## Examine the Proposal Logic Chain

For each major proposal under comparison, show a concise **Proposal Logic Chain**: action → intermediate change → problem improvement → Needs Goal. At material links, distinguish supporting facts, user preferences, and unverified assumptions. A correlation, missing evidence, or plausible story leaves causation unproved.

Recheck first principles when a proposal forms, a contradiction appears, new evidence arrives, and before settling the summary. Examine which links are necessary, which intermediate steps could disappear, what upstream cause might explain the problem, and whether a constraint is only an existing practice. Keep root-cause hypotheses open to counterexamples.

Where there is a substantive opportunity, offer a **Fundamental Alternative** that reaches the same Needs Goal by addressing an upstream cause or removing a step. Explain the link it could improve and compare both proposals against the same success criteria and constraints, including trade-offs. Directness alone does not establish superiority; without a defensible alternative, say so rather than inventing one.

## Validate and converge

Find the uncertain assumption with the greatest effect on the decision. Propose the smallest check that could distinguish plausible outcomes, and explain how each observation would change the choice. A proposed or unrun check remains unverified.

Move to the summary once the key links have reasonable support, the main alternatives have been compared, and remaining questions have clear validation directions. Material contradictions stay visible with their effect on the decision; they prevent a settled conclusion. If the user asks to stop, provide a partial summary with unresolved items and end the interview.

## Deliver the Needs Summary

Provide the **Needs Summary** in the conversation, sized to the task: problem, users and context, confirmed goal, scope, constraints, success criteria, reasoning, assumptions, alternatives, selection rationale, and unresolved questions with validation directions. Distinguish confirmed content from suggestions and unknowns; do not fill missing information with invented decisions.

Confirmation of this summary establishes shared understanding only. It creates no implementation, file-writing, or tracker authority. Preserve explicit authorization already given for subsequent work and return to its applicable flow without asking for the same approval again. With no follow-up instruction, the conversation summary completes this skill.
